01 · Your Space Guide

What Are Meteoroids?

Meteoroids are small natural objects made of rock, metal, or both, traveling through space. Many come from broken pieces of asteroids or comets.

What Are They Made Of? 🧪

Rock: Meteoroids contain rocky minerals and other solid materials.

Metal: Some contain metals, especially iron and nickel.

Rock and Metal: Others contain a mixture of rocky and metallic materials.

Where are they found? 🌌

Meteoroids travel through space throughout our Solar System. Many orbit the Sun and can be found along different paths between planets.

How Do They Move? 🛰️

Meteoroids move through space, usually orbiting the Sun. Their paths can change because of gravity or collisions with other objects.

Did you Know? ☄️

When a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere and produces a bright streak of light, the light phenomenon is called a meteor, also known as a shooting star.

02 · Know the Difference

Meteoroid vs. Meteor vs. Meteorite

These three words describe different things. The main difference is where the object is and what happens to it.

Meteoroid

IN SPACE

A small rocky or metallic object traveling through space before it enters an atmosphere.

Meteor

IN THE ATMOSPHERE

The bright streak of light produced when a meteoroid enters an atmosphere and heats the surrounding gas.

Meteorite

REACHES THE GROUND

A piece of a space object that survives its journey through the atmosphere and reaches the ground.

Easy Way to Remember

Meteoroid = in space. Meteor = streak of light in the atmosphere. Meteorite = a piece that reaches the ground.

03 · Key Characteristics

Important Characteristics

Meteoroids can differ in size, composition, speed, and origin.

Size 📏

Meteoroids are generally smaller than asteroids. They can range from tiny grains to larger rocky fragments.

Composition🧪

They may be made of rocky minerals, metals such as iron and nickel, or a mixture of materials.

Origin 💥

Many meteoroids are fragments created when asteroids or comets break apart or collide with other objects.

04 · Solar System Comparison

Meteoroids vs. Other Space Objects

Meteoroids, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets differ in their composition, size, appearance, and movement.

Meteoroids vs. Asteroids

Feature Meteoroids Asteroids
Size Generally smaller fragments Generally larger space objects
Composition Rock, metal, or both Mostly rock and metal
Origin Many come from broken asteroids or comets Some produce smaller fragments through collisions
Movement Travel through space, usually orbiting the Sun Orbit the Sun

Why Do Scientists Study Meteoroids?

Studying meteoroids and meteorites helps scientists understand the history of our Solar System.

Clues About the Early Solar System

Meteoroids preserve materials from the early Solar System. Studying them helps scientists learn about its formation.

Understanding Planet Formation

Meteorites provide clues about the materials that helped form planets billions of years ago.

Understanding Possible Risks to Earth

Scientists study near-Earth objects to understand their paths and assess potential risks to our planet.

Did You Know? ☄️

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago. Some meteorites contain materials dating back to its earliest history. Scientists examine meteorites on Earth to learn about the materials present when the Solar System formed.
